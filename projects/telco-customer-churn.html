Telco Customer Churn Analysis

End-to-end churn analysis on 7,043 telco customers — cleaning, exploratory analysis, and predictive modelling to answer: which customers are most likely to leave, and why?

Python Pandas Scikit-learn

01 Business problem

Telco companies lose recurring revenue every time a customer leaves. The core question: which customers are most likely to churn, and what's driving it — so retention effort can be targeted rather than blanket.

02 Background & dataset

IBM Telco Customer Churn sample dataset (publicly available via Kaggle and IBM).

  • 7,043 customers, 21 features

08 Key insights

  • Overall churn rate: ~27% — roughly 1 in 4 customers leave
  • Contract type is the strongest churn driver: month-to-month customers churn at ~43%, vs ~11% for one-year and ~3% for two-year contracts
  • Early tenure is the danger zone — churn concentrates in the first 12 months
  • Best model: Logistic Regression, ~80% accuracy on the held-out test set
  • Top churn drivers (Random Forest): TotalCharges, tenure, MonthlyCharges, fibre-optic internet, electronic check payment

09 Business recommendations

  • Convert month-to-month customers to annual contracts with discount incentives — the single biggest retention lever
  • Strengthen first-90-day onboarding, since early-tenure customers are the highest-risk group
  • Review fibre-optic pricing and value perception — the premium product shows premium churn
  • Use the model's churn scores to target retention offers at high-risk, high-value customers

10 Challenges & what I learned

The churn class is imbalanced (fewer churners than non-churners), which affects recall on the minority class. Addressing this with SMOTE or class weights is flagged as a future improvement, alongside testing gradient boosting models (XGBoost, LightGBM) and deploying churn scoring for CRM integration.
